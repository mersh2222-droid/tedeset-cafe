# Dishes & Drinks

Covers prepared food, bread, and coffee/tea menu items — for
`apps/studio/schemas/menuItem.ts` descriptions and any café menu copy.
Ethiopian and Eritrean cuisines share most core dishes; note the few naming
differences called out below.

## Stews ("wat" / Eritrean "tsebhi")

- **Doro wat** — chicken stew in berbere sauce with hard-boiled egg; the
  festive-occasion dish in both cuisines (Eritrean: "tsebhi derho").
- **Misir wat** — spiced red lentil stew (berbere-based); vegan, common
  fasting-day dish.
- **Shiro wat** — spiced chickpea/broad-bean flour stew; vegan staple, often
  the everyday "what's for dinner" dish.
- **Key wat / sik'sik wat** — beef in berbere sauce.
- **Alicha wat** — the mild, turmeric-based (non-chili) version of any of
  the above stews.
- **Gomen** — collard greens, usually mildly spiced.
- **Atkilt (wat)** — cabbage, carrot, and potato stew, mild.
- **Zigni** — Eritrean term for a hot berbere-based meat stew, close cousin
  to key wat.

## Meat dishes outside the stew format

- **Tibs** — sautéed/pan-seared cubed meat (beef, lamb, goat) with onion,
  garlic, rosemary, and berbere or awaze; served sizzling.
- **Kitfo** — minced beef, traditionally served raw or lightly warmed
  ("leb leb"), mixed with mitmita and niter kibbeh; often with a side of
  gomen and ayib (fresh cheese).
- **Gored gored** — cubed raw beef, plain or with mitmita/awaze dip.
- **Kilwa** — Eritrean dried/cured spiced beef.

## Vegan / fasting ("tsom") dishes

Ethiopian Orthodox and Eritrean Orthodox fasting periods exclude meat and
dairy; shiro wat, misir wat, gomen, atkilt, and salads are the fasting-menu
staples — useful to flag on a menu as "vegan" rather than only "vegetarian."

## Breads and sides

- **Injera** — fermented teff flatbread, the utensil and plate for nearly
  every dish; sour, spongy texture from natural fermentation (helped by
  gesho starter).
- **Dabo** — round, mildly sweet steamed/baked bread.
- **Ambasha** — sweeter, spiced flatbread, often decorated with a
  cross-hatch pattern, associated with celebrations.
- **Himbasha** — Eritrean spelling/variant of ambasha.
- **Kolo** — roasted barley (sometimes with peanuts/chickpeas), a snack.
- **Ayib** — fresh, mild farmer's cheese, common alongside kitfo and gomen.

## Coffee & tea (café menu)

- **Ethiopia is the origin of coffee (buna)**; the traditional coffee
  ceremony (roasting green beans, grinding, brewing in a **jebena** clay pot,
  served in small cups over three rounds — abol, tona, baraka) is a cultural
  centerpiece worth referencing in café copy, separate from the espresso-bar
  drinks.
- Standard espresso-bar items on the current menu (`Coffee.csv` /
  `Coffee_menu.ndjson`) — Americano, Cappuccino, Latte, Macchiato, Cold Brew,
  Double Espresso — are conventional espresso drinks; describe them normally
  (roast, milk, temperature) rather than assigning them invented Ethiopian
  cultural framing they don't have.
- **"Ethiopian Roast Coffee"** on the menu should be described by roast/origin
  (Ethiopian-grown beans, roast level) — confirm the specific origin
  (e.g. Yirgacheffe, Sidamo, Harar) with the owner before naming a region,
  since that's a factual sourcing claim.
- **Spiced/chili tea drinks** (e.g. "Chili Latte Tea") likely reference
  Ethiopian spiced tea traditions (ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, sometimes
  berbere-adjacent chili) — confirm the actual recipe before describing
  specific spices used.

## Writing menu descriptions

- Name the dish, its main ingredient(s), the spice blend used, and heat
  level (mild/alicha vs. spicy/berbere-based) — that's usually enough for a
  2-sentence description.
- Call out vegan/vegetarian status explicitly; it drives real ordering
  decisions for fasting-period customers.
- Don't invent a spice list for a dish/drink not covered here or confirmed
  by the owner — say what's known and flag what needs confirming.
