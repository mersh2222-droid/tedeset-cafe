# Spices & Pantry Staples

Ethiopian/Eritrean cuisine builds almost everything from a small set of
spice blends layered onto simple bases (legumes, grains, meat, greens). This
file covers the blends and single spices most likely to appear in the
grocery catalog (`apps/studio/products.csv`,
`apps/studio/products_Baking_Seasoning.csv`).

## Core spice blends

- **Berbere** — the signature blend: dried chili, garlic, ginger, fenugreek,
  basil, korarima (Ethiopian cardamom), rue, ajwain, and warm spices
  (cinnamon, clove, allspice). Ground into red powder or sold as a paste.
  Base seasoning for almost all "wat" (stew) dishes and for tibs.
- **Mitmita** (also seen as "Mitimita") — hotter and finer than berbere:
  bird's-eye chili, cardamom, cloves, salt. Used as a table condiment for
  kitfo and gored gored (raw/seared beef), and mixed into niter kibbeh as a
  dip.
- **Shiro powder** — roasted, ground chickpeas and/or broad (fava) beans
  blended with berbere spices. The base of shiro wat, one of the most common
  everyday (and vegan/fasting) dishes.
- **Awaze** — a paste made from berbere thinned with wine, water, or oil;
  served as a dipping condiment alongside meat.
- **Alicha (Alch) spice mix / "kimem"** — the mild counterpart to berbere:
  turmeric, ginger, garlic, and aromatics *without* chili. Used for "alicha
  wat," the non-spicy stew style. A catalog line like **"Alch Kimem"** is
  this mild seasoning blend, not a chili blend — don't describe it as spicy.
- **Bread spice / "Kerbi" or "Kimem" bread blends** — a warm-spice mix
  (cardamom, cinnamon, clove, nutmeg) used in dabo/ambasha bread and in
  spiced tea.
- **Niter kibbeh** — clarified butter slow-cooked with garlic, ginger,
  onion, and spices (koseret, besobela/holy basil, cardamom). The standard
  cooking fat for wats; sold as jarred ghee-like product.
- **Kibe / Ethiopian butter** — plain or spiced clarified butter, precursor
  to niter kibbeh.

## Single spices and aromatics commonly stocked

- **Korarima** — Ethiopian/false cardamom, distinct from green cardamom;
  common in berbere and coffee ceremonies.
- **Black cardamom, green cardamom** — used in spice blends, tea, and coffee
  ceremony incense/flavoring.
- **Fenugreek ("abish")** — seeds or ground powder ("**abish duket**" —
  *duket* = "powder" in Amharic). Used in berbere, in porridge (kinche/genfo),
  and traditionally as a postpartum/nursing remedy tea.
- **Black cumin ("tikur azmud")** — nigella seed, used whole in bread and
  some spice mixes; distinct from regular cumin.
- **Caraway seed** — used in bread and some spice blends; catalog may list
  both a generic and an "Ethiopian" sourced version — same spice, different
  supplier/origin, describe accordingly rather than as different products.
- **Ajwain / koseret / besobela (holy basil)** — herbs used in niter kibbeh
  and spice blends; typically sold dried.
- **Rue ("tenadam")** — pungent herb, traditional coffee-ceremony and
  digestive-tea addition.
- **Cinnamon, clove, ginger, turmeric, black pepper** — general pantry
  spices used across both cuisines and in coffee/tea preparation; when the
  catalog lists an "Ethiopian" or "Mid East" sourced variant, describe the
  origin/sourcing difference, not a different spice.

## Grains, legumes, and flours

- **Teff** — the staple grain (tiny, gluten-free), fermented into injera
  batter; also sold as flour.
- **Barley** — used for porridge (genfo/kinche) and roasted snacks (kolo).
- **Masoor dal / red lentils** ("**Masoo Dal**" in the catalog) — split red
  lentils, the base of misir wat (spiced red lentil stew), a staple
  vegan/fasting dish.
- **Kinche / genfo grains** — cracked wheat or barley for porridge dishes.
  A catalog item like **"Aja Kinche"** is a branded/prepared cracked-grain
  porridge mix — confirm the brand name with the owner before asserting more
  than "cracked grain porridge mix" in copy.
- **Gesho** — dried leaves/bark (hops-like bittering and fermenting agent)
  for brewing injera starter, tella (traditional beer), or tej-adjacent
  drinks. Sold as "Ethiopian Gesho Powder."

## Serveware vs. food — don't confuse the two

Several catalog names look food-related but are equipment, filed under
"Traditional & culture" alongside jebena (coffee pots), rekbot (coffee
stands), and cup/teacup sets:

- **Mitad / injera griddle** ("ALMI Digital Mitad") — electric injera-making
  griddle, not a food item.
- Items like **"Kitfo Dist"** are very likely a serving plate/dish for kitfo
  (a "dist" is a plate/tray) rather than the dish itself or a spice — check
  the category and price point (serveware is priced like housewares, not
  like a spice jar) before writing a food description for it.

## Verification rule

If a name doesn't match anything above and isn't a widely-documented
Ethiopian/Eritrean/East African grocery term, say so plainly and ask
Merhawi rather than guessing at a translation or ingredient list — these
become public allergen and ingredient claims once published.
